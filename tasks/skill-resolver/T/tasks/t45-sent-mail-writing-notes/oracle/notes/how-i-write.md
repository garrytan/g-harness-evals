# How Maya writes email

Built from the 14 sent emails in sent/: 9 internal (lanefield.com), 5 external. Small corpus, so frequencies are rough. Every external email ends with the same block "Maya Okafor | Operations, Lanefield / +1 415 555 0142"; that is the mail client's signature, not a sign-off, and drafts must not include it.

## Register map

- **Internal (9 of 9):** all lowercase, no greeting, 1 to 3 short lines, direct verdicts. "yep, move the 7am to 9." / "can't, i'm at the yard till 3." / "meh. the routing is fine but the driver app is clunky."
- **External (5 of 5):** sentence case, "Hi <first name>," greeting, short paragraphs, polite but firm, ends with a concrete question or ask. "Can we talk Monday or Tuesday for 20 minutes?" / "Could you confirm by Thursday?"
- **Length tracks stakes:** routine internal replies are one line; a complaint to a carrier (05) or a contract fix (10) is still under 80 words.

## Openers & sign-offs

- **Internal openers:** none in 9 of 9; the first word is the answer ("yep", "can't", "not yet", "approved", "looks good", "meh"). "quick one:" opens 1 of 9 announcements.
- **Internal sign-offs:** a lone "m" on its own line in 7 of 9; nothing in 2 of 9 (07, 12); "thx" before the "m" once (11).
- **External openers:** "Hi <first name>," in 5 of 5, often followed by "Thanks ..." (3 of 5). "Thanks for sending the rate sheet." / "Thanks for the demo last week."
- **External sign-offs:** "Best," then "Maya" on the next line in 5 of 5.

## Rhythm

- Internal: one idea per line, line breaks instead of paragraphs, often two short sentences. "not yet. greg wants a call, i'll do tues."
- External: 2 or 3 paragraphs of one or two sentences; the problem or context first, the ask last, as a question. "The draft looks right except section 4.2 ... Can you send a corrected version?"
- Lists only when there are several asks: numbered for questions to vendors (02), dashes for internal asks (11).

## Vocabulary & phrases

- "fwiw" in 2 of 9 internal emails. "fwiw the carrier said friday is tight anyway."
- "lmk", "+1", "thx", "tues", "thurs", "fri" internally; never externally.
- Says no plainly and gives the reason and a next date: "i'd say no for this peak and look again in feb." / "We've decided not to move forward before peak; the timing is too tight ..."
- Hedges with "i'd" + verb rather than "maybe" or "perhaps": "i'd cut the vendor review to 30 min", "i'd start pricing a backup carrier".

## Punctuation & formatting quirks

- No exclamation marks in any of the 14 emails.
- "->" as a connector internally (03): "moves to 2pm starting next week -> carriers asked".
- Internal emails never capitalize "i" or sentence starts; names stay capitalized ("Tom") except in quick replies ("greg").
- No bold, no emoji, no links except a drive path in parentheses.

## Hard rules

- Internal: lowercase, no greeting, answer in the first word, sign with "m" or nothing.
- External: "Hi <first name>," then "Best," / "Maya". Never more than about 80 words.
- Every external email ends on a concrete ask with a day or date.
- Never an exclamation mark.
- Never include the "Maya Okafor | Operations, Lanefield" block; Gmail adds it.

## Anti-patterns

- No "I hope this finds you well", "Just checking in", "I'd be happy to", "Please don't hesitate to reach out".
- No enthusiastic adjectives ("great", "amazing", "excited").
- No bullet-point answers to a plain question; lists only for several asks.
- No sentence-case or greetings in internal mail; no "fwiw", "lmk" or lowercase in external mail.
- No long context paragraphs before the point.
