# How Maya writes email

Based on the 14 emails in sent/ (9 to colleagues at lanefield.com, 5 to people outside the company).

## Overview

You write very differently depending on who you are writing to. To colleagues you are terse and informal: lowercase, no greeting, the answer first. To outside people you are polite, structured and short, and you always end with a clear ask.

## Greetings and sign-offs

- **Colleagues:** no greeting at all. You sign off with just "m" most of the time, sometimes with nothing.
- **External:** "Hi <first name>," and "Best,\nMaya". Your mail signature (name, Operations, Lanefield, phone) follows; Gmail adds that automatically.

## Sentence style

- Internal emails are written all in lowercase, one to three short lines.
- External emails use normal capitalization, two or three short paragraphs, and put the question at the end.
- You use numbered lists for multiple questions to vendors and dashes for internal action items.

## Words and phrases you use

- "fwiw", "lmk", "+1", "thx", "quick one:"
- Abbreviated days: "tues", "thurs", "fri"
- Opinions start with "i'd ...": "i'd cut the vendor review to 30 min"
- You say no directly and suggest a later time: "i'd say no for this peak and look again in feb."

## Formatting

- No exclamation marks anywhere.
- "->" to connect cause and effect.
- No bold, emoji or HTML formatting.

## Things you avoid

- Filler openers like "I hope this finds you well".
- Over-enthusiastic language.
- Long explanations before getting to the point.

## Example drafts

Internal: "yep, works for me. lmk if the carrier pushes back.\nm"

External: "Hi Greg,\n\nThanks for the update. Can you confirm the new pickup window by Friday?\n\nBest,\nMaya"
