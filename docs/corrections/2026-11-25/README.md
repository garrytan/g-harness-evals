# Correction of 2026-11-25: review-real-bugs' code-reading judges fail a third-model check, so its false-blocker numbers are descriptive

**We learned that the judges behind review-real-bugs' false-blocker numbers agree poorly with a third model from another family, though most passed checks against labels from their own two families (C2's did not).** Gemini 4 Neon, labelling blind with the same tools and brief as the published labellers, agreed with the re-judge's primary judge (GPT-6 Astra) at kappa **0.37 [0.15, 0.56]** on 99 findings, under the 0.6 bar. Stage 2's C1 and C3 judges scored 0.29 and 0.46. Astra's failure depends on how its can't-tell rulings count: the counted reading, frozen before any label and the one the published checks use, counts a can't-tell as a disagreement; dropping them (0.61) or reading them as defect (0.62) would clear the bar. C1 and C3 fail under all three readings.

**You should read stage 1's verdict as "KEEP (recall only; false blockers on bug PRs unmeasured)", and its +0.04 false blockers per PR and plain-8's 0.27 against 0.06 per bug PR as descriptive.** That is the eval's own rule for a failed judge check. Every recall number stands: Neon agreed with both matching judges at kappa 0.98, and stage 1's +18.5 points of blocking recall is unchanged.

This correction is about the review-real-bugs run ([stage 1 report](../../benchmarks/2026-11-review-real-bugs/report.md), [stage 2 report](../../benchmarks/2026-11-review-real-bugs/stage2/report.md)). It applies R126: a model judge on code counts only once it agrees with Gemini 4 Neon's labels at kappa 0.6 or more. The two reports gain dated lines that point here; their original figures, results and trial records are unchanged. The stage 2 report also gains C2's matching check, which was run and passed but never published.

## What changed, in one list

1. **Stage 1 verdict (corrected).** KEEP becomes **KEEP (recall only; false blockers on bug PRs unmeasured)**. The rule is `decideVerdict` in [scripts/review-bugs/score-mc.ts](../../../scripts/review-bugs/score-mc.ts): when the adjudication judge check fails, false blockers on bug PRs are descriptive and only decoy false alarms guard the verdict. Stage 1 carried this same label before the re-judge.
2. **Stage 1 guards (now descriptive).** False blockers per bug PR, /review minus plain: +0.04 [0.00, +0.09] counted, +0.12 [+0.04, +0.20] with can't-tell as a false blocker. Unchanged as numbers, but no longer validated.
3. **Stage 2 false blockers (now descriptive).** C1's judge (Astra) and C3's (Astra) fail, so plain-8's 0.27 false blockers per bug PR against /review's 0.06, and C3's, are descriptive. C2's were already descriptive. **No stage 2 verdict changes:** none of its rows is a KEEP, and their labels (LOSS, CUT-CANDIDATE, FIX-METHOD) come from recall, plus, for C2's FIX-METHOD, the decoy false-alarm guard (upper bound +0.57 per decoy PR), which the eval's rule keeps after a failed judge check (see "Decoy false alarms" below).
4. **Recall (unchanged, now checked).** Neon agrees with the stage 1 matcher (Astra, also C1 and C3's matcher) at kappa 0.98 [0.95, 1.00] on 114 items, and with C2's matcher (Opus 5.5) at 0.98 [0.95, 1.00] on 119.

- **How to read it.** Bold is the counted reading, fixed before any label was paid for: the judge's can't-tell is its own category, so it counts as a disagreement, as in the published checks. The 95% range is a bootstrap over items (2,000 resamples, seed 20261125). The bar is 0.6.
- **Why the published checks passed and this one fails.** The published checks scored each judge only on findings where the Astra and Opus labellers agreed (153 of 204 for the re-judge, 216 of 277 for C1), which leaves out the hardest ones. On a random draw of all findings, the judges' can't-tell rulings and their not-defect rulings that Neon calls real defects pull kappa down. Re-run on this sample, the published check gives 0.59 for the re-judge's Astra.
- **Where they differ.** Mostly on the judges' can't-tell: on the re-judge sample Astra ruled 77 defect, 11 not-defect and 12 can't-tell, Neon 86, 13 and 1. Where both ruled, Astra's and Neon's defect and not-defect calls split both ways (3 findings one way, 5 the other), so this check gives no direction for Astra's counted false-blocker numbers. C2's Opus 5.5 is different: it ruled not-defect on 23 findings Neon called defects, and the reverse on none.
- **Neon is not ground truth either.** On C1, Neon agrees with the two published labellers' agreed labels at only 0.51. All three readers disagree there.
- **Decoy false alarms.** The eval's rule keeps them as a guard even after a failed check, and this correction follows the rule. They were ruled by the same judges, so this check doesn't validate them either. Every reading the stage 1 report gives (+0.27, -0.07 and +0.03 per decoy PR) is inside the +0.5 limit, so the recall-only KEEP holds in each.

## 2. How the labels were made

- **Labeller.** Gemini 4 Neon (`google:gemini-4-neon`, thinking level medium, at most 16,000 output tokens), through our metering proxy under a ledger lease. Gemini plays no other role in this eval.
- **Blind.** It never saw a judge's ruling, a labeller's ruling or an arm name. Matching items: the published label passes' own packets, one request per packet ([scripts/review-bugs/third-model-label.ts](../../../scripts/review-bugs/third-model-label.ts) `match`). Adjudication items: one read-only tool session per finding, with [tool-judge.ts](../../../scripts/review-bugs/tool-judge.ts)'s labeller role unchanged (its brief, prompt, tools and budget); only the transport is new.
- **Sample.** All 120 items of each matching check. For adjudication, 100 findings per check drawn with seed 20261125 (re-judge 100 of 204, C1 100 of 277, C2 100 of 452, C3 all 99). Ids: [third-model/sample.json](third-model/sample.json).
- **Known-answer anchors, frozen before any paid label.** 20 matching items (10 findings that quote the fix and restate the bug, want match; 10 spelling complaints at a path the fix never touches, want no-match) and 20 adjudication items (10 findings that restate the unit's known bug, want defect; 10 claiming the PR deleted a line it in fact adds, want not-defect). The bar was 18 of 20 right per kind and 8 of 10 per side, and at most 20% abstentions per check. The frozen sha256 were `b8931e46` (matching key, byte-identical to the committed file) and `59f46e39` (adjudication key); the committed adjudication key blanks each item's `line`, a line of private-repo code, so it hashes to `d8102045`. The labels were made by third-model-label.ts as of commit 1e306747 (sha256 `18707dc8`, the frozen version); every committed label passes the later input checks. The current labeller also stops starting new reads if the proxy cannot confirm a read’s charge and request count, and marks the cost incomplete (`cost_complete: false` in `run.json`). Neon got **20 of 20 on both kinds** and abstained on 0.8% to 5% of the items in each of the six checks, so the Grok 4.7 fallback was not needed. The anchors' sha256 and the bars were written down before any paid label call.
- **One block outside the six checks.** Stage 1's matching packets also hold 120 adjudication items, so Neon labelled them in the same request. They belong to stage 1's blind adjudication check, which already failed (kappa 0.012) and was replaced by the re-judge, so no number here rests on them. Neon abstained on 40 of the 120 (33%), above the 20% limit, and agreed with that judge at kappa -0.01. [sensitivity.json](sensitivity.json) keeps the block under `match.stage1.adjudicate`.
- **Cost.** $121.77 at list price over 11 leases: anchors $3.65, matching $19.39, adjudication $98.71, one transport smoke $0.01. A tool session cost about $0.24. The committed rows' own costs add to $121.38 (the smoke has no rows). The $0.38 gap is in the re-judge and C2 checks, each run over two leases after the first run stopped at its lease limit: it paid for requests whose rows were not kept and were redone in the second run.
- **Timing.** The labels ran on 2026-11-25 from a branch that adds Neon's list price to the proxy's price table (#612), before that change reached main.

## 3. Rebuild it

`bash scripts/corrections/reproduce-2026-11-25.sh <unpacked archive> <scratch dir> --check` rescores the committed labels in [third-model/](third-model/) against the judges' rulings and the labellers' answers, and compares the result with [sensitivity.json](sensitivity.json). Those rulings and answers are in the run's private raw archive `raw-review-real-bugs-2026-11-stage2-grading` ([docs/archive/index.json](../../archive/index.json)), so the rebuild needs it unpacked. No model is called.

The Neon labels themselves are committed: one row per read with its ruling, tool calls, tokens and cost (`adjudicate-*.jsonl`, `anchors-adjudicate.jsonl`), and one row per packet with its labels (`match-*.jsonl`). Their prompts hold findings and repository text from the private archive, so they are not.
