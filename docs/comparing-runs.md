# Comparing runs

harness-evals v0.0.12. This page says how two cells or two runs are compared in this repository, which scripts do it, which flags they take, what each one writes under a run's `results/`, and the exact commands each counted run's report names. The statistics kit is [runner/stats/](../runner/stats/) (vendored from gbrain-evals, MIT, see [gbrain-evals-conventions.md](gbrain-evals-conventions.md)); the rules it serves are items 9 and 10 of the [trial contract](methodology.md). Nothing here calls a model: every script reads committed receipts and runs keyless.

## The terms, in order of need

**A trial, a cell, a task cluster.** A trial is one harness attempt at one task; a cell is one harness, one model, one configuration over a task set at a fixed number of repetitions ([methodology.md](methodology.md), Terms). The five (or ten) trials of one task in one cell are not independent of each other: the same task, the same world, the same oracle. So every interval and test on this page treats the task as the unit that varies, which the statistics kit calls a cluster. Resampling or flipping a cluster moves all of its trials together, and five repetitions of one task never count as five tasks.

**A paired comparison.** Two cells are compared trial by trial: the trial on task `si-03`, repetition 2, in cell A is set beside the trial on `si-03`, repetition 2, in cell B, and the difference (B minus A, 1 or 0 for pass) is recorded for that pair. The mean of those differences, in pass-rate points, is the estimate. Pairing removes the part of the noise that comes from the tasks themselves: a hard task is hard for both cells, so it drops out of the difference instead of widening the interval. A pair whose trial ended as `model_error`, `infra_error` or `exam_fault` on either side is left out of the pairing with its count published (methodology item 9), because that trial never measured the harness.

**The task-clustered paired bootstrap interval.** The 95 percent interval around the paired difference comes from resampling tasks, not trials: the script draws tasks with replacement, carries every pair inside each drawn task along, recomputes the mean difference, and repeats 10,000 times from a fixed seed (7). The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of those 10,000 means are the interval. Because whole tasks are drawn, an effect concentrated in two tasks gives a wide interval, which is the honest reading: the run knows about two tasks, not sixteen.

**The exact sign-flip p.** The test asks: if the two cells were really the same, how often would a split this lopsided appear by chance? Under that null, each task's summed difference is as likely to be positive as negative, so the script flips the sign of each task's sum in every one of the 2^g combinations (g tasks; exact up to 20 clusters, Monte Carlo with the same 10,000 draws above that) and counts how many combinations give a mean at least as far from zero as the observed one. A task whose summed difference is zero contributes nothing to the mean, so only the tasks that moved count: when exactly six tasks move and all six move the same way, the two-sided p is 2 x 0.5^6 = 0.031, and five one-way tasks give 0.0625, above 0.05. That is the sign-flip bar the preregistrations quote as "six task clusters all one way"; with all 16 clusters moving one way the p is 2 / 2^16, about 0.00003.

**Holm correction.** A report that tests three harness pairs, or six ladder rungs, runs several tests on the same data, and the chance that at least one of them falls below 0.05 by luck grows with their number. Holm's step-down adjustment sorts the p values, multiplies the smallest by the number of tests, the next by one fewer, and so on, never letting an adjusted value fall below the one before it, and caps at 1. A difference is only reported as a difference on the adjusted value. The family the adjustment runs over is named in each output file (`holm_family`) and in the preregistration; a family of one leaves p unchanged, which is why two of the ladder reports compute the family of two by hand and say so.

**The minimum detectable difference.** Before a run, and again from its own trials afterwards, the question is: how large a paired difference could this design have seen? `scripts/paired-mdd.ts` takes each task's pass rate in the cell, assumes under the null that both arms share it and are independent given the task, so one paired difference has variance 2p(1 - p), sums that over the design, applies the same cluster-robust standard error the comparison uses, and multiplies by the normal quantiles for two-sided alpha 0.05 and power 0.8 (1.96 + 0.84). The result is in pass-rate points. A task every harness passes 5 of 5, or fails 0 of 5, contributes no variance, so a run near the ceiling reports a small number: it says what the design detects on the tasks that already vary, not what it would take to break a task nobody fails. The reports print the preregistered figure and the restated one from the run's own cells side by side.

**"Not distinguished at N tasks".** This is the sentence a report uses when a comparison does not meet its preregistered bar, and it is deliberately not "the same" or "a tie". Every preregistration since the held-out run carries a decision sentence of the shape: a pair is reported as different only when the Holm-adjusted two-sided p is below 0.05 and the task-clustered interval excludes zero; otherwise "not distinguished at 16 tasks" (or "at 16 tasks by 10 repetitions", or "at this size"), with the interval shown. A cell at 0 or 5 of 5 on every primary task is uninformative, not a tie. The sentence lives in the preregistration, not in the code: `compare-run.ts` prints intervals and p values and says so in its header, and the report applies the sentence to them. A reader who wants the verdict reads the preregistration's sentence and the `comparisons*.json` row together.

## The statistics kit, runner/stats

- [paired.ts](../runner/stats/paired.ts): `pairObservations` (both sides must hold the same item ids and the same eligibility on each item, or the pairing refuses rather than shrinking the denominator), `clusteredPairedDelta` (the bootstrap interval and the sign-flip p above; it also reports the cluster-robust standard error, the naive one, and the design effect between them), `exactMcNemar` (wins, losses and the exact two-sided p over the discordant pairs), `minimumDiscordantForSignificance` (the six-cluster bar), `holmAdjusted`, `normalQuantile` and `powerNote`.
- [gates.ts](../runner/stats/gates.ts): the four gate kinds a preregistered comparison family can declare (exact, noninferiority, superiority, exploratory) with Holm over the confirmatory ones. The harness-evals preregistrations state their bar as a decision sentence in prose and the scripts report intervals and p values, so this file is available but no counted run has been judged through a gates family file.
- [exact.ts](../runner/stats/exact.ts): Clopper-Pearson binomial bounds. [fixed-sequence.ts](../runner/stats/fixed-sequence.ts): hierarchical testing in a preregistered order, kept apart from Holm. [rows.ts](../runner/stats/rows.ts): row to observation mapping and the eligibility rule.
- [sign-flip.ts](../runner/stats/sign-flip.ts), [task-means.ts](../runner/stats/task-means.ts), [sizing.ts](../runner/stats/sizing.ts): shared with gstack-evals, vendored from it unchanged (the margin-shifted sign-flip test for non-inferiority, pairing on per-task means, and sizing arithmetic).
- **Which kit analyses a run.** Kit v2 (2026-11-04) takes gstack-evals' fixes: no p when every paired difference is zero, `superiorityP` as the exact one-sided sign-flip p, failures scored by the comparison's direction, and the gate-order fixes in `gates.ts` ([runner/stats/README.md](../runner/stats/README.md)). A run is analysed with the kit at its preregistration commit unless a dated amendment names another ([kit.ts](../runner/stats/kit.ts)), and `compare-run.ts` records the kit it used as `stats_kit` in each comparisons file. The old-kit and new-kit rerun of every published comparison is [stats-kit-v2-rerun.md](stats-kit-v2-rerun.md).

## The scripts

Every script takes `--results <dir>` pointing at a directory that holds `trials.jsonl` and `manifest.json` (under `docs/benchmarks/<date>-<name>/results` for a committed run). All write beside `trials.jsonl`. Flags below are read from each script's header; a flag not listed here is not one the script takes.

### scripts/summarize-run.ts

`bun scripts/summarize-run.ts --results <dir> [--regraded] [--seed 7]`. Per cell: n, passes, pass rate with a task-clustered bootstrap 95 percent interval (10,000 draws, seeded), consent violations, approval stops and how many were valid, terminations, cross-scope read failures, refused proxy requests, USD (total, median per trial, per success), tokens and median wall time; then a per-task by cell pass table, the failed-predicate counts, a per-family table, a secondary-predicate table, and the gbrain usage and ladder tables when a trial carries a `gbrain` record. Trials ending `model_error`, `infra_error` or `exam_fault` are excluded from the scored rows and counted in an Excluded column. A cell whose scored trials are all `product_probe` tasks gets no pooled rate (`probe: true`, a descriptive table under `probes`). Writes `summary.json` (`summary.regraded.json` with `--regraded`, read from `trials.regraded.jsonl`) and prints Markdown; the `summary.md` files under several runs are that printout saved by the operator.

### scripts/compare-run.ts

Four ways to call it, one estimator:

- `bun scripts/compare-run.ts --results <dir> [--regraded] [--families a,b] [--seed 7] [--draws 10000]`: every pair of cells in the run, B minus A in pass-rate points, interval, sign-flip p, Holm-adjusted p across the pairs, clusters and pairs. Writes `comparisons.json`, or `comparisons.<families joined by ->.json` when `--families` restricts the tasks (so `comparisons.scope_isolation-shared_channel-org_security-handoff.json` is the output of `--families scope_isolation,shared_channel,org_security,handoff`), with `.regraded` before `.json` under `--regraded`.
- `bun scripts/compare-run.ts --results <run A> --against <run B> [--families ...]`: pairs each harness's cell in A with its cell in B on the same tasks and repetition indices, for a model effect or a between-run stability check. Writes `comparisons.against-<run B's run id>[.<families>].json`. It needs exactly one matching cell per harness on each side; the company brain report records that it could not pair against the gbrain arm, which has two `none` cells per harness.
- `bun scripts/compare-run.ts --results <dir> --baseline <cell>[,<cell>...] [--families ...]`: only each baseline cell against every other cell on its model, B minus the baseline, so cells of different models are never paired. One baseline per model. A pair whose trial ended as `model_error`, `infra_error` or `exam_fault` on either side, or is missing on either side (a repetition only one cell ran included), is excluded on both sides, and each comparison lists the excluded pairs with their reasons. Writes `comparisons.json` (named for `--families` and `--regraded` as in the first mode) with a `baselines` key. Written for `harness-zero-select-2026-11`, where each ablation is paired with `zero-full` on its model. Each comparison also carries `endpoints`, computed on the pairs it kept by [scripts/baseline-endpoints.ts](../scripts/baseline-endpoints.ts): the pass-rate interval's half-width (`pass_half_width`; the noise floor F when the pair is two copies of one cell); the paired cost-per-trial difference from each trial's proxy-priced spend (`usd_per_trial`, the same estimator as pass rate); total spend B over A (`usd_per_trial_ratio`); each side's spend over its passes and B's over A's (`usd_per_success`); each side's cached input tokens over its input tokens and B minus A (`cached_share`); and the broken and fixed tasks (`tasks`: broken when the task passes in more than half of its kept pairs in the baseline and in at most half in B, so 3 of 5 against at most 2 of 5, or 2 of 3 against at most 1 of 3; fixed the other way). A pair with no usage record on either side leaves the cost and cache endpoints only, listed under `cost_excluded`. Every ratio interval is a task-clustered percentile bootstrap: each draw resamples tasks with replacement and recomputes the ratio from the drawn tasks' sums, with the same seed, draw count and draw sequence as the pass-rate interval, so both resample the same tasks in each draw; a draw with a zero denominator (no pass on one side of a cost-per-success draw) leaves the interval null with the count. The file also carries `repricing`, one entry per baseline cell: every forwarded line in the proxy usage files beside `trials.jsonl` (`proxy-usage.ndjson` and each shard's `<shard>.proxy-usage.ndjson`) for the cell's scored trials, priced again with every input token at the plain input rate at the metering ledger's price for the line's model, over what the lines cost, with that ratio's interval and the cache-read share by the gap since the trial's previous request (first request, at most 300 seconds, longer). A trial whose lines do not add up to its usage record's tokens, or with a line that has no price or no `actual_usd`, is left out with its reason. A line the proxy charged its reservation (an answer with no or partial usage, or a failed call) adds what it was charged to both sides, counted under `charged_reservation`.
- `bun scripts/compare-run.ts --results <dir> --pair gbrain [--design arm|ladder] [--families ...] [--require-exposure]`: the within-run gbrain designs. The arm design pairs each gbrain cell against the one cell without gbrain for the same harness, configuration and model, over two scopes (the primary families, by default scope_isolation, shared_channel, org_security and handoff, and all tasks), Holm across the harness pairs of one model. The ladder design pairs each install rung against its ladder neighbour within harness and model on four endpoints (pass, gbrain use, a cross-scope read via gbrain, and exposure via gbrain: a gbrain read in any turn that returned a record that turn's requester may not read), Holm per endpoint over the comparisons of one harness on one model, with a descriptive none table and paired cost, wall and input-token rows labelled with the shards the two cells ran on (from `shards.json`). The design is chosen from the manifest (a verbs, docs, installer or embedded cell makes it the ladder) and `--design` overrides. Writes `comparisons.gbrain[.<families>].json`; the `comparisons.gbrain.md` files are the saved printout. The ladder output records under `exposure` whether that endpoint is ready, with its problems; `--require-exposure`, accepted only with the ladder design, exits 2 after writing when it is not (a scored trial with no exposure value, an unmapped returned page or fact, or a grader commit that is unknown, dirty or not the commit `shards.json` records for every cell; [gbrain-arm.md](gbrain-arm.md), "The paired comparison and the report scripts").

Defaults are seed 7 and 10,000 draws; every report that names them says the same two numbers. One known difference between the paths, recorded in [TODOS.md](../TODOS.md): without `--pair` or `--baseline` the script does not exclude `model_error`, `infra_error` or `exam_fault` trials, while `--pair gbrain`, `--baseline` and `summarize-run.ts` do, so `comparisons.json` can show a cell with more pairs than `comparisons.gbrain.json` does (the company brain run's OpenClaw scoped cell: 100 against 95).

### scripts/paired-mdd.ts

`bun scripts/paired-mdd.ts --results <dir> [--families a,b] [--alpha 0.05] [--power 0.8]`: for each cell, the minimum detectable paired difference defined above over the primary families and over all tasks, with the pass rate, the tasks at 0 or 5 of 5, the null standard error and the sign-flip bar. `bun scripts/paired-mdd.ts --ladder [--results <dir>] [--alpha 0.05] [--power 0.8] [--reps 10]` does the same per ladder endpoint for the 20-task design, taking its rates from the gbrain arm's Haiku trials or from the directory given. `--reps` restates the figure for a design at that many repetitions at each task's measured rate; the header lists it under `--ladder`, and the arm path reads it too (the v0.2 held-out report used `--reps 5`). Prints Markdown only; `mdd.md` and `paired-mdd.md` under the runs are the saved printout, and the preregistrations quote the figure with that path as its receipt.

`bun scripts/paired-mdd.ts --project --results <dir> --pair <told-live cell>:<none cell> [--pair ...] [--families a,b] [--tasks 36] [--reps 3] [--alpha 0.025] [--holm-family 2] [--power 0.8] [--threshold 15]` is the v0.4 power gate (internal-task): from fresh frozen-dev rows it projects, per harness, the minimum detectable difference for told-live against none at a target design. Unlike the modes above, it uses the measured difference between the arms, not a shared rate under the null. The estimator is named in the output: the task-clustered variance of the per-task differences, split into a between-task part and a within-task binomial part (each task needs at least two scored trials per arm), and scaled to the target design as sqrt((between + within / repetitions) / tasks). The test is two-sided at 0.025 per opening divided by the two harnesses of the Holm family, with power 0.8. Beside each figure it prints the fewest repetitions at the target task count, and the fewest tasks at the target repetitions, that bring the figure to the threshold. It reads the five v0.4 primary families unless `--families` says otherwise, and leaves out model, infrastructure and exam errors.

A side of a `--pair` may join several cells of one harness with `+` (`--pair a1+a2:b1+b2`), and their tasks are pooled into that side. The v0.4 frozen-dev read keeps one cell per family and level, so each harness's pair joins its frozen primary-family cells.

`bun scripts/paired-mdd.ts --h10 --task-set <set> --tasks <id,...> [--split dev] [--reps 1] [--repetition-pairs 4] [--placement-pairs 3] [--base-repetition 0.95] [--base-placement 0.9] [--alpha 0.025] [--power 0.8] [--sims 1000] [--seed 7] [--family-b <rate,...> [--family-b-alpha 0.0071]]` is H10's power check (internal-task). No data exists yet for the one-time hook on frontier models, so it simulates from stated rates instead of measuring. It reads each task's member turns and repeat turns from the task's own turn order (`structuralSessions` in `scripts/instruction-followed-h10.ts`) and finds the smallest effect, on a one-point grid, whose simulated power under the scorer's exact two-sided sign-flip test over tasks reaches `--power`. It prints the per-task structure and one row per contrast: family A's repetition (route-once against route-hook, repeat turns only) and placement (route-once against route-system, all member turns), plus one family B row per `--family-b` control rate. A task with no repeat turn adds no repetition cluster. Prints Markdown only.

### scripts/secondary-endpoints.ts

`bun scripts/secondary-endpoints.ts --results <dir> [--seed 7] [--draws 10000] [--families a,b]`: the same pairing as `compare-run --pair gbrain` over five secondary endpoints (cross-scope read by any route, cross-scope read via gbrain, hidden-fact leak, consent violation, continuity pass), Holm within one model per endpoint. Written for the v0.2 held-out run. Writes `secondary-endpoints.json`.

### scripts/regrade.ts

`bun scripts/regrade.ts --results <dir> [--cells a,b] [--out <file>]` (default `<dir>/trials.regraded.jsonl`): grades every trial again from its stored evidence (`snapshot.json`, `events.jsonl`, `turns.jsonl`) with the grader at the current commit and writes one line per trial with the original and new grade, consent violation, approval stop and predicates, plus the grader commit. Nothing in the run directory is rewritten: a regrade is a labelled second reading, never a replacement of the preregistered grader's record, and every report that shows regraded numbers keeps the as-recorded ones as primary. It finds each trial under `--results` (so a moved or unpacked bundle works), reads `snapshot.json`, `events.jsonl` and `turns.jsonl` plain or gzipped, and exits 0 when every selected trial regraded unchanged, 1 when any trial could not be regraded or its grade, consent violation or approval stop changed (one line per trial names what moved), and 2 on a usage error. `scripts/replicate.ts` uses it as the grader check of a rerun (docs/replicate.md).

For v0.4 calibration rounds, `bun scripts/v04/round-results.ts ... --regraded` builds a descriptive `round-<n>.regraded.json` from `trials.regraded.jsonl` (the recorded round file stays the one the rule replayed), and `bun scripts/v04/cal-report.ts` puts both readings side by side with task-clustered intervals and steps each family through the regraded rounds until its path splits from what ran (used by the v0.4 calibration report, amendment 4).

### scripts/taxonomy.ts

`bun scripts/taxonomy.ts --results <dir> [--regraded] [--tasks tasks/multiuser] [--world world/seed/world.json] [--calibration <hand-labels.jsonl>] [--annotations <file>] [--trial <id>]`: labels every failed required predicate and forbidden effect with an observed failure mode from the receipts (which oracle sources were read inside the turn, whether the reply declines or says it found nothing, which writes the world refused), one line of evidence each, from a closed label list tried in a fixed order. Labels are observations, not diagnoses, and no pass rate depends on them. Writes `taxonomy.json` and `taxonomy.md` (`taxonomy.regraded.*` with `--regraded`); `--calibration` reports agreement with a hand-labelled sample, by default `taxonomy-calibration.jsonl` beside the results directory; a run folder's `taxonomy-annotations.jsonl` names task or grader defects the report already records, labelled `exam_defect`.

### scripts/reconcile-provider.ts

`bun scripts/reconcile-provider.ts --results <dir> [--results <dir> ...] [--write] [--tz America/Los_Angeles] [--api-key-id <apikey_...>] [--provider-fixture <file>]`: the proxy-side spend check, always: the priced lines of `proxy-usage.ndjson` per UTC day and model against the same spend read from `trials.jsonl` and from the lease ledger, with the largest difference between the views and a per-day sheet in the shape of the provider console. The provider side runs only when `ANTHROPIC_ADMIN_KEY` is set, which no counted run has had, so every committed reconciliation says the provider check was not performed. Writes `spend-reconciliation.json` only with `--write`; the per-shard `<shard>.spend-reconciliation.md` files under the v0.2 runs are the printout saved per shard, because the script reads one ledger per directory.

### scripts/merge-shards.ts

`bun scripts/merge-shards.ts --out <results dir> --shard <name>=<shard results dir> [--shard <name>=<dir> ...] [--partial [--vm-lost <cell> ...]] [--skipped-by-rule <cell>[=<reason>] ...] [--cells a,b]`: builds the one results directory the scripts above read from a sharded run (whole cells on separate machines, CREW.md, Parallelism). It takes the first shard's `manifest.json` and refuses unless every other shard's agrees once `created_at` is removed, concatenates `trials.jsonl` in the manifest's trial order (a trial in two shards, in none, or outside the manifest is a refusal, as is a cell split across shards), copies each shard's `trials/`, keeps every shard's ledger, proxy log, usage log, run summary and run log under `<name>.<file>` (tokens never copied), and writes `shards.json` mapping each cell to its shard with the shard's machine, commit and image ids. When the shards carry `set-worlds.json` (the run's set-world pins), every copy must be identical, and the merge refuses otherwise and copies it once. `compare-run.ts --pair gbrain` reads `shards.json` to say whether two paired cells ran on the same machine, because wall time across machines is confounded with the machine. With `--partial`, a run that stopped on its cap merges from what exists: each cell with unstarted trials must end on its own `cap_stop` record, backed by a cap refusal at or after that trial's `started_at` in its shard's usage log, and the unstarted trials go to `unstarted.json`, never backfilled ([runner/README.md](../runner/README.md), "Sharded runs"). `--vm-lost <cell>` (with `--partial`) accepts a cell whose VM was lost with no trial merged at all, its shard left out, and lists it under `vm_lost` in `unstarted.json`; a named cell with a recorded trial is refused. `--skipped-by-rule <cell>[=<reason>]`, with or without `--partial`, accepts a planned cell that the preregistration's own conditional rule did not run (`harness-zero-select-2026-11`'s `no-eh-sol`): it must be in the manifest, have no recorded trial and not also be `--vm-lost`; its trials are neither missing nor unstarted, and `shards.json` lists it under `skipped_by_rule` with the reason and its planned trial count. A missing cell that is not named still refuses. Nothing is written until every check passes, and an `--out` that already holds `trials.jsonl`, `unstarted.json` or a proxy usage log is refused. The company brain run was merged by hand before this script existed, and its report says so and notes that its `shards.json` follows the script's shape; the gbrain arm's report names no merge script either.

`--cells a,b` merges a run that ran only some of its preregistered cells (a v0.4 calibration round or the frozen-dev read, whose rule picks the subset): only those cells' trials are planned, a picked cell's trial in no shard, any trial of another cell and a shard without a `preflight-clean-arm.json` that records `clean: true` are refusals, and the merged `manifest.json` holds only those cells, with a `subset` block giving the preregistration's full cell and trial counts. Every shard's own manifest is kept whole.

### scripts/v04/clean-arm-audit.ts

`bun scripts/v04/clean-arm-audit.ts audit --results <dir> [--out <file>]`: per cell and arm, the trials whose files (tool traces, turn requests and responses, adapter logs, the world snapshot) name a gstack path, mention gstack, or mention `.capy/drive`. An arm with a trial that names a gstack path is contaminated (CREW.md, "Clean no-gstack and no-brain arms"); exit 1 when any arm is. It exits 2 before counting when a trial recorded in `trials.jsonl` has no directory. A trial whose termination counts toward a rate and lacks an agent trace for any turn cannot be shown clean: it is listed under its cell as `unaudited_scored` with the turns that have no trace, its cell and arm are reported `shown_clean: false` with the count (apart from `contaminated`), and the audit finishes for every arm and exits 1. An agent trace is the harness's own per-turn record, which the adapters write only when a turn runs: Hermes's stream-json events (`logs/turns/turn-NNNN.hermes.jsonl`) or OpenClaw's `--json` envelope (`logs/turns/turn-NNNN.openclaw.json`), each optionally `.gz`, holding at least one JSON record. Every turn the runner sent (`turns/turn-NNNN.request.json`) needs its own trace, and a trial needs at least one. `logs/ready` (written before any turn), the adapter, harness and docker logs, the runner's turn requests, the turn responses, `events.jsonl`, `snapshot.json`, `grade.json`, `usage.json`, `reconcile.json` and `turns.jsonl` do not count. A trial that ended `infra_error`, `model_error` or `exam_fault` without a trace for every turn counts toward no rate, so it is listed under its cell as `unaudited_unscored` instead of stopping the audit. A file it cannot read, such as a corrupt `.gz`, exits 2 naming the trial and the file. Run it on the merged results: a committed results tree keeps only the files its preregistration names, so it usually holds no agent trace. gstack matches only where it starts a word (`gstack`, `gstack-ship`, `.gstack`, `gstack/SKILL.md`), never inside one such as `sigstack.ph`, in the audit and the preflight alike. A harness's own bundled `SKILL.md` files have no gstack path segment and do not count. `preflight` is the before-run side, which `scripts/run-shard.sh --clean-arm-preflight` runs on each shard: no Capy drive and no gstack install on the machine or in the harness images.

## Reading a comparisons row

A row in `comparisons*.json` or the saved `comparisons*.md` carries: the two cells, passes on each side, B minus A in pass-rate points with the 95 percent task-clustered interval, the two-sided sign-flip p, the Holm-adjusted p, the number of task clusters and the number of pairs (after exclusions, which the gbrain outputs list under `excluded` with a reason). The report then applies the preregistration's decision sentence to the adjusted p and the interval. A lower interval end at exactly zero, as in the installer hook run's use endpoint, is what the bootstrap gives when every discordant pair is on one side: the difference cannot be negative, so the interval's width comes only from how many pairs sit on that side.

## Limits of the method, as the reports record them

- A run near the ceiling is uninformative, not a tie: in the Sonnet held-out run 13 of 16 primary tasks were 5 of 5 for all three harnesses, so the comparison rested on three tasks and the sign-flip bar could not be reached. The ceiling sentence in each preregistration exists for this.
- The bootstrap interval and the sign-flip p can disagree when few clusters move: an interval above zero with four one-way clusters still gives p 0.125, because four one-way clusters cannot reach 0.05 under an exact sign flip (the installer hook run's Hermes pass row). The reports print both and apply the sentence to both.
- The minimum detectable difference is computed from the tasks that vary. It does not say what it would take to break a task every harness passes.
- Between-run comparisons (`--against`, the stability sections) are descriptive by preregistration: two runs differ in time, machine and sometimes commit, so no sentence is applied to them.
- `compare-run.ts` without `--pair` and the other paths disagree on exclusions until the TODOS item is closed; read the pairs column.
