# Methods and fine print

harness-evals v0.0.29. This page holds the full statements behind the plain summaries in [README.md](../README.md): how to read the numbers, the complete comparison text for the harnesses and for gbrain, the complete limits, how every harness runs, what the world and the tasks are, what paid work costs, and what a results directory holds. Every paragraph here is the README text as it stood before the 2026-10-28 rewrite, moved word for word with its links re-based to this directory; nothing here changes a number, and every figure still traces to the report and receipts its row names.

## How to read the numbers

- Each run is dated by the UTC days its trials ran (three directories carry the day before their trials). Dollar figures are the reports' own, shown to the cent where a report gives more decimals; the table's spend column is the settled or usage figure against the lease, and the details keep the committed and lane figures where a report gives them.
- Intervals are 95 percent and clustered by task. A paired difference is B minus A over the same task and repetition. p values are exact sign-flip tests over task clusters with Holm correction within the run's preregistered family.
- "Not distinguished" is the preregistered decision sentence, not a tie, and a cell at ceiling or floor is uninformative rather than equal.
- Every figure is copied from the report named in its row and the receipts under that run's `results/`; [docs/receipts-manifest.json](../docs/receipts-manifest.json) carries each run's preregistration commit, trial count, ledgers and the sha256 of its `trials.jsonl`.
- Status says whether a run was its held-out set's first counted opening; the details name the set and which counted opening of that set it was, numbered in the order the preregistrations merged on main by `runner/heldout/openings.ts` (`bun runner/heldout/openings.ts` prints the ledger). Only a first opening is confirmatory; a later opening of the same set is dev, because whoever designed it had already seen that set's results, so its intervals hold for those tasks but cannot confirm the claim ([Corrections](../README.md#corrections), 2026-10-28). A pilot or a probe run uses the dev split.

It leaves out H10's $5.50 first launch and smoke measurement (settled $3.89 plus an open $1.61 reservation), whose ledgers sit in the run's `smoke-measurement/` folder, which the receipts manifest does not read. Every dollar is the metering proxy's list-price meter; the provider-side check has never been performed (Known limits).

## How the harnesses compare, in full

With the model fixed, OpenClaw, Hermes Agent and QM over Pi were not distinguished on whether they got the multi-user work right, in any run that compared them: on the v0.1 held-out tasks with Sonnet the three sat at 77, 73 and 71 of 80 with 13 of 16 tasks at ceiling, with Haiku at 64, 63 and 56 of 80 and every Holm-adjusted p above 0.4, and on the harder v0.2 tasks the within-model differences are descriptive only ([m3-heldout](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-24-m3-heldout/report.md), [sensitivity](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-24-m3-sensitivity-haiku/report.md), [v02-heldout](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-v02-heldout/report.md)). Of the runs that compared harnesses, only m3-heldout and v02-heldout were the first opening of their held-out set; the sensitivity and QM inner-harness runs are later openings of v0.1 and read as dev, so their Haiku and QM-engine figures below support engineering decisions here, not a confirmed claim ([Corrections](../README.md#corrections), 2026-10-28). Swapping Sonnet for Haiku moved every harness by 13 to 19 points, more than any harness gap, though only OpenClaw's gap survives the preregistered sign-flip test after Holm. This tier contrast is background only: these evals now test frontier models only (Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol). What is distinguished is cost and time: QM's Pi engine, not distinguished from the others on pass, ran at about a third of the wall time and tokens of OpenClaw (27 s and $0.06 against 70 s and $0.16 per trial at the median on Sonnet; 12.5 million input tokens against 32.3 million over 100 trials), with Hermes between them, and the ordering held on Haiku. The safety predicates held for everyone on v0.1: no consent violation and no leaked DM fact in the 600 held-out trials without a brain, one cross-scope read (Hermes). Where the harnesses did lose trials, the failure taxonomy puts most of it in one mode, a channel the answer sat in that the harness never opened ([docs/report-v0.1.md](../docs/report-v0.1.md), "Where the failures were"). QM's two inner engines were not distinguished on pass either, and the Claude Agent SDK engine cost 2.7 times as much per trial ([qm-inner-harness](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-qm-inner-harness/report.md)); QM's product probes held its memory and sandbox claims per scope where OpenClaw and Hermes, which make no such claim, shared a DM file across members ([qm-probes](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-qm-probes/report.md), [docs/qm-findings.md](../docs/qm-findings.md)).

## How gbrain compares, in full

gbrain is not a harness; it is a knowledge brain served beside the world tools, and the question in ten of the runs above is what each harness does with it. Every gbrain run above except v02-heldout is a later opening of a held-out set, so each is a dev result that supports engineering decisions inside this program but not a claim confirmed on unseen tasks; the installer-hook and scoped runs retested a revised candidate on the same tasks that prompted the revision, the verbs run re-tested the ladder's verbs rung on the same tasks, the fix run retested gbrain#7744 on the memory run's tasks it was built against, and none of these effects has yet been confirmed on a held-out set it was not developed against ([Corrections](../README.md#corrections), 2026-10-28). Installed and unmentioned, the harnesses almost never used it (a gbrain call in 2 of 600 trials of the arm, 2 of 199 scored trials on the ladder's `mcp` rung; [finding](../findings/2026-11-01-untold-gbrain-unused.md)) and nothing moved but trial length and, for QM, input tokens. One brain-first clause in the per-turn preamble turns use on in about nine of ten trials; gbrain's own installer file, placed where the harness reads it, produced no use until its revision added a per-turn hook, after which use was 80 of 80 in both harnesses ([installer](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-gbrain-installer/report.md), [installer-hook](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-gbrain-installer-hook/report.md)). Where that rule enters decides whether the bot checks gbrain before it answers, each harness compared only with its own control on the 13 public v0.3 dev tasks (dev): on OpenClaw only the per-turn hook differed from tool descriptions alone (160 of 160 member turns made a gbrain call before the first reply, against 5 for `AGENTS.md`, 1 for a listed skill and 1 for tool descriptions alone), and on Hermes the system prompt setting, `SOUL.md` and the hook each differed (144, 138 and 156 of 160, against 5 for a listed skill and 0 for tool descriptions alone); on the unscoped bot-visible brain these routes went with cross-scope reads, and pass was 32 points lower on the OpenClaw hook (descriptive, not tested; [instruction-route](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-31-anatomy-instruction-route/report.md)). Told, the harnesses gained the two cross-channel retrieval tasks they had failed without a brain and lost trials to a new failure: keyword `search` over a bot-wide brain returns pages from channels the requester is not in, which the grader counts as a cross-scope read; on the v0.2 held-out tasks that read rose in every one of six pairs while the pass effect stayed within noise, and with semantic search on it cost 48 to 51 points. The fixes measured so far work by narrowing what a call can return: the seven memory verbs, where `recall` returns one page, had zero cross-scope reads in 394 trials, and per-request scoping by the member's grant (gbrain#7481) had zero in 197 trials against 76 of 99 and 72 of 100 unscoped, with pass up 61 and 65 points ([company-brain](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-company-brain/report.md), [gbrain-scoped](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-gbrain-scoped/report.md)). A member brain that fills during the scenario, each member reading under their own grant, read across members in 0 of 450 live trials, and every DM fact the tap saw returned belonged to the requester, while the pass effect again stayed within noise on Sonnet (the largest, Hermes +23 [+0, +53] over 40 pairs, carried by two memory tasks, Holm p 1.000; [gbrain-memory](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-26-gbrain-memory/report.md)). Rerun at gbrain#7744's fix, which returns a newer correction ahead of the fact it corrects, the memory run's twelve cells on its eight continuity and corrections tasks were again not distinguished on pass, while `cr-05`, the task the fix was built against, went from 0 of 5 in every Sonnet live cell of the memory run to 4, 5 and 1 of 5, with every none cell at 0 of 5 ([gbrain-memory-fix-b](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-28-gbrain-memory-fix-b/report.md)). No run has yet shown gbrain raising pass rate on a primary endpoint by an amount its design can distinguish from zero; the detectable differences were 8.5 to 17.7 points. Design, pins and the per-trial sidecar are in [docs/gbrain-arm.md](../docs/gbrain-arm.md).

## Known limits, in full

- **Spend is proven proxy-side only.** For every counted run the proxy's usage log, the per-trial `usage.json` sums and the lease ledger are reconciled against each other (`bun scripts/reconcile-provider.ts`), and each report explains what they do not share: a reservation a machine sleep left open counts as committed in the ledger but not as usage (the gbrain arm's Haiku ledger carries $6.95 committed against $6.17 settled), and a restarted trial's forwarded call appears in the usage log but not in `trials.jsonl`. That the provider billed the same has never been checked, because the check needs an Anthropic Admin API key and on 2026-10-26 the decision was not to provide one, so every figure stays the list-price meter and says so ([docs/methodology.md](../docs/methodology.md) item 3 as amended 2026-10-24, docs/budget-ledger.md).
- **The repository is private.** The publishing audit passes and the checklist records what remains for the owner ([docs/publishing-checklist.md](../docs/publishing-checklist.md)); until it is public, "anyone can regrade" means anyone with access.
- **Three runs carry a declared deviation from their preregistration.** The company-brain run's OpenClaw scoped cell ran its last 65 trials under runner code at `e278fb0` instead of the preregistered `6b67de1` after a machine restart, regraded identically under both ([report](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-company-brain/report.md), opening section); the qp-07 rerun's preregistration and results were squash-merged in one commit, so main's history cannot show the order, and the order check prints it as a warning ([report](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-qm-probes-qp07/report.md)); the v0.2 held-out run raised one shard's lease during its cell, did not use its second amendment's output-token cap, and resumed two shards after machine sleeps at the recorded commit ([report](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-25-v02-heldout/report.md), "Deviations"). Every other run states no amendments or deviations, or records what happened to the run itself (machine restarts, preflight relaunches, a discarded shard under a dated amendment) without a change to what was measured; all of them are in [docs/decisions.md](../docs/decisions.md) and the reports.
- **Hermes makes free model-discovery probes.** About 27 refused requests per trial (about 2,700 per 100-trial cell), recorded as upstream noise at $0 and not as harness errors; QM and OpenClaw make a few utility calls of their own, so request counts are not comparable one for one across harnesses.
- **One fixed model per run, CLI `serve` sessions driven by the runner rather than each harness's native Slack ingress, one bot token with no delegated access, and tasks the adapters' author has now seen several times.** The v0.1 held-out set has served ten counted runs and is at ceiling on Sonnet, and the v0.2 held-out set has served three, so only each set's first counted opening is confirmatory; that label is qualified because the bodies of both sets sit in the repository, sealed only by digest ([Corrections](../README.md#corrections), 2026-10-28); the scheduled family is uninformative by construction because no harness fires on the simulation clock.
- **The harness-versus-harness Sonnet figures in the gbrain arm compare machines as well as harnesses**, and three QM trials there ended `harness_error` from a port collision and are counted as failures by the scripts; treating them as excluded changes no sentence ([report](../docs/benchmarks/2026-10-24-gbrain-arm/report.md)).

## How every harness runs

Every harness runs headlessly in `serve` mode: the runner drives each member's messages through the world's Slack mock on a simulation clock, and the harness answers through the one world-MCP server registered in its own configuration ([docs/adapters.md](../docs/adapters.md)). One bot token, no delegated per-member access, and the same preamble for every harness ([docs/methodology.md](../docs/methodology.md), the trial contract).

Claude Code and Codex run the same way from v0.0.42 ([harnesses/claude-code](../harnesses/claude-code/README.md), [harnesses/codex](../harnesses/codex/README.md)). Codex 0.165.0 speaks only OpenAI's Responses API, which Anthropic does not serve, so Codex runs on an OpenAI model and is compared only with OpenClaw and Hermes on that same model; Claude Code and Codex are never compared with each other.

## The world and the tasks, in full

The world is a simulated company, Pemberline, seeded from gbrain-evals' `messy-company-v1` dataset (a generated corpus; [world/seed/MANIFEST.json](../world/seed/MANIFEST.json) names its inputs and digests): 18 members, 13 channels, 148 files, 42 mails, calendars and private notes, served per trial by one process that logs every read and write ([world/](../world/README.md)). A task is one or more members talking to the bot in channels and DMs; the grader reads the world's state and its event log afterwards, never the bot's own account of what it did ([docs/tasks.md](../docs/tasks.md)). The multi-user slice has four families every harness can attempt (scope isolation, two members in one shared channel, an org-level rule such as a finance figure that needs approval before it is relayed, and a handoff that creates or sends something) plus a scheduled family no harness can do yet. Task set v0.1 is 10 public dev tasks and 20 held-out tasks sealed before the counted runs ([tasks/multiuser/README.md](../tasks/multiuser/README.md)); task set v0.2 adds continuity, synthesis, corrections and approval-relay families, 15 dev and 15 held-out tasks at the last counted run, with its own seal ([tasks/multiuser/v0.2/README.md](../tasks/multiuser/v0.2/README.md)). A trial passes when every required predicate holds and no forbidden effect occurred; the three counts reported beside pass are consent violations (a finance figure or outside mail that should not have gone out), leaks (a DM fact in another member's conversation) and cross-scope reads (a channel or file the requester cannot see, read directly or through gbrain).

## What paid work costs

Paid work needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` in the proxy's environment only and a ledger lease under a cap; the metering proxy refuses a call without a lease ([runner/README.md](../runner/README.md)). A counted run also needs its preregistration merged before its first trial, which `bun runner/prereg/prereg.ts` checks against main's history rather than the runner at run time; the gbrain check below writes a scratch preregistration of its own and is never counted. What a run costs, from the ledgers above: a 300-trial run of three harnesses on 20 tasks was $1.84 on Haiku and $35.67 on Sonnet; a 400-trial four-cell Haiku run with gbrain was $3.36 to $3.47; the 900-trial v0.2 held-out run on both models was $76.60 by proxy usage ($77.04 committed). The paid gbrain check, `bun scripts/gbrain-check.ts --gbrain <ref> --harness openclaw,hermes`, runs a scratch dev-split cell against a committed baseline for about $0.35 ($0.3526 for 40 trials; [docs/gbrain-arm.md](../docs/gbrain-arm.md), "Checking a gbrain commit"). Sharded runs go to Ubicloud VMs through `scripts/ubicloud/fanout.sh` ([docs/ubicloud.md](../docs/ubicloud.md)).

## What a results directory holds

- `docs/benchmarks/<run>/` holds each counted run: `PREREGISTRATION.md` and `prereg.json` (committed before the first trial; `bun runner/prereg/prereg.ts` checks the order), `report.md`, and `results/` with `trials.jsonl` (one record per trial: termination, grade, effects, predicates, usage, paths), `summary.json`, the comparison files, `proxy-usage.ndjson` (one line per proxied request, no bodies, no keys), the lease ledgers (one per shard), `taxonomy.json` where run, and per trial `events.jsonl`, `turns.jsonl` with the exact prompts, `snapshot.json.gz`, `usage.json`, `reconcile.json`, `grade.json` and `logs/adapter.log`. Harness state, workspaces, raw harness logs and per-trial tokens are not committed ([runner/README.md](../runner/README.md), the receipts recipe).

## How preregistration order is checked

**Before the public cutover, the order of preregistration and results is attested privately, not publicly verifiable.**
- The public repository's history before the cutover was rebuilt from the private repository's squash merges. Commit dates in it are self-asserted, so they prove nothing on their own.
- [docs/benchmarks/prereg-ledger.json](benchmarks/prereg-ledger.json) is the record. For each experiment merged before the cutover, it gives the private preregistration commit, its merge date and PR number, and the sha256 of the preregistration and of the result manifest (one `sha256  path` line per result file).
- `bun runner/prereg/prereg.ts --public` checks each of those experiments against the ledger: the hashes must match and the history date must agree. It labels them "ordering attested privately, not publicly verifiable".
- **After the ledger is written, the check is strict.** Every other experiment, and any result file added after the ledger commit, needs its preregistration commit to be a strict ancestor of its results in public history. A declared deviation does not excuse it, and a result file that only a merge brought in fails. Each commit counts as the commit on main's first-parent line that brought it in, so a preregistration and results that arrive in one merge fail. In the export, a file's position comes from its first add in the private history, never from where it first appears in public, so holding results out of one export can't make them look later. A receipt's attestation names a private commit, which the export maps to its public commit through its commit map; the private history must also show that the commit contains the preregistration, because commits on one branch share a public commit. The one way past the same-merge rule is a named exception in the private `publication/prereg-exceptions.json`, for one experiment and an exact path set, applied only after the private history shows the order it states and printed in the export report with its reason. Today it covers the frontier-harness extension results.
- `--public` prints an attested experiment's declared deviation next to its label.
- **The public copy's CI runs `--public` on every pull request and every push to main.** Default mode (`bun runner/prereg/prereg.ts`) checks each receipt's attestation against commits the public copy never holds, so the `prereg` job runs it only in the private repository. A receipt's attestation names a private commit. The public CI reads it through `docs/benchmarks/prereg-attestations.json`, which the export writes into its last commit: each such private commit and the public commit it's checked at. The check then confirms that public commit contains the preregistration, and labels the attestation "attestation mapped by the public export, which checked its private ancestry". The map also carries each same-landing exception from `publication/prereg-exceptions.json` (its experiment, path globs and the public commit of its merge), so the public CI waives the same files the export did. A missing map fails the job; it never skips. The export's prereg-public gate (`--public` with the export's commit map and the private clone) is still the full verifier, and it runs before every push, followed by a run exactly as the public CI runs it.
- `bun scripts/prereg-ledger.ts` writes the ledger from a full clone of the private main, once every scrub is committed, just before the export. It refuses if any experiment's preregistration merged after the cutover, so no private experiment is left out.
- **The ledger is written once.** If more than one commit in the history changed it, `--public` fails every experiment, so a later edit can't move the cutover or add an entry. The export drops earlier versions of the file from the rebuilt history and adds the final one in a single commit.

## Headroom: which tasks can tell arms apart

- **What it is.** A task every arm of a run passed on every try, or every arm failed on every try, can't show a difference between arms. The rest are the tasks that can tell arms apart. `summarize-run.ts` ends with a Headroom section that counts them and gives the per-task, per-arm pass table; `bun scripts/headroom.ts --results <dir>` prints the same section for any results folder. Every results PR includes it.
- **The gate.** `scripts/matrix-analysis.ts` (its "higher" calls) and `scripts/frontier-harbor/analyze.ts` (its within-15 calls) refuse a call when fewer than 10 of the two arms' paired tasks can tell the run's arms apart (`min_paired_tasks` in a frontier spec), or when on those tasks the reference arm leaves less room than the smallest difference the preregistration named. The call then reads "can't tell: no room to improve" with the counts. Room for a "no worse than" call is the reference arm's passed share on those tasks; for a "better than" call it is the losing arm's failed share. Both are over all paired tasks, so they compare directly with the margin.
- **Why the run's set and not the pair's.** Counting per pair would treat two arms that agree on a task as a task with no room, so a harness that matches its home harness would be refused for agreeing. A task some third arm fails shows the task can separate arms. In the frontier-harness run, counting per pair would have refused 4 of its 5 published "within 15 points" calls: those pairs keep 8 or 9 tasks and 10.6 to 14.4 points of room. The fifth, Pi against Codex on GPT-6.1 Sol, keeps 12 tasks and 16.7 points, so it would stand. Counted across the run, all five keep 28 or 29 tasks and 40 to 46 points.
- **What it does not change.** The gate runs after Holm, so the preregistered family, its p-values and every other call stay as they are. A matrix spec names its smallest difference in `smallest_difference`; without it only the count is checked, and the output says so. The spec files of runs published before the gate (listed with their sha256 in `scripts/headroom.ts`) keep the output they were published with; an edited copy gets the gate.
- **The lean-harness run under this rule.** The lean-harness head-to-head (harness-zero-h2h) has no gate in its own analysis. Counted across all 15 of its arms, 11 of its 20 tasks can tell the arms apart. Its two "no worse than" calls (QM and Codex against the lean harness) each keep those 11 tasks, with 27 and 32 points of room against its 10-point margin, so both would stand.
- **Other headroom rules in the repo.** matrix-analysis's drop-difference rule (H3) keeps its own 8-task minimum over tasks informative in both drops, a different question already published. `instruction-followed-h10.ts` calls a family with no headroom when its control follows the instruction more than 25% of the time, which is a rate rule for one experiment. Neither is changed.
- **What comes next.** This gate fires after a run has spent. The bigger saving is a headroom check before a run spends (the control arm between about 20% and 80% on a pilot, at least 10 tasks that can tell arms apart) and a two-stage design; neither is built yet.
