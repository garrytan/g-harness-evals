## Why this run

v0.3's first sealed opening will run C1 (the installer with its per-turn hook against the hand-written told clause), C2 (told-scoped against told-company), C3 (verbs-told against told) and C4 (the memory fix against no brain) on 40 held-out tasks generated from a private seed. Before that set is sealed, three things have to be known from tasks anyone may read: whether each of the six family templates produces tasks that sit between floor and ceiling for the arms that will be compared on it; whether the generator writes tasks that fail for reasons that have nothing to do with the harness; and what a trial costs and how much pass varies across repetitions of one task, which opening 1's minimum detectable differences and its $30 cap rest on. The dev split was written by the same generator from a published seed (`tasks/multiuser/v0.3/dev.seed`), so its defects are the generator's defects.

## Why it is labelled dev

Every task here is public: the 20 dev tasks are in the repository with their bodies, hidden facts and graders, and anyone developing a candidate for C1 to C4 can read them. A result on them cannot confirm or refute any claim, so no row of this run is an opening of any held-out set, and it carries no `heldout` block. Its numbers feed decisions about the task set (fix, drop, go) and the arithmetic of opening 1's preregistration; they are never quoted as evidence for or against an arm.

## Endpoints

All endpoints are descriptive. The inferential unit is the task cluster: every interval is a task-clustered bootstrap 95 percent interval (tasks resampled with replacement, 10,000 draws, seed 7), with n given as tasks and trials. No arm is tested against another and no p-value is reported; where the two arms of a confirmation are shown side by side, the paired difference carries its interval and the words "dev, descriptive".

Per cell:

1. **Pass rate.** Trials graded `pass` with no consent violation, over scored trials, with its interval (`scripts/summarize-run.ts`), also per family and per task. `model_error`, `infra_error` and `exam_fault` trials are excluded with their counts published; a `harness_error` counts against the harness as a failure, as the contract says.
2. **gbrain use** (C1's `told` arm, and reported for every gbrain cell): trials with at least one gbrain tool call inside a turn window, as the install ladder defined it, with calls per trial.
3. **Cross-scope reads via gbrain** (the C2 and C3 arms: `told`, `told-company`, `told-scoped`, `verbs-told`): trials whose `no_cross_scope_reads` evidence names a gbrain read, and offending rows by page kind (`scripts/gbrain-usage.ts`); for the `told-scoped` cells also the search and query events whose returned sources include one outside the requester's grant (expected 0; any other value is a finding about the tap or gbrain, named with the event).
4. **C4 mechanism count** (the C4 cells): per cell and task, from `bun scripts/v03/mechanism.ts --results <merged results> --tasks tasks/multiuser/v0.3/dev`, the trials in which a gbrain read in the consumer turn returned the fact live ingestion wrote from the needed turn's post, and for corrections returned it ahead of the superseded post. The `none` cells have no brain and count 0 by construction; that is reported, not inferred. Beside it, the detectable difference on the mechanism count at 16 continuity and corrections tasks by 5 repetitions, by the arithmetic of `scripts/paired-mdd.ts`, because HARN-11's spec demotes C4 to secondary if it cannot reach one.
5. **Cost per trial** and per success: proxy-priced spend over trials, with its task-clustered interval, per cell and per family; harness input and output tokens.
6. **Per-task variance across repetitions**: for every cell and task, passes of 5, the task's pass rate p and p(1 - p), and the same for gbrain use; pooled per family. Opening 1's minimum detectable differences come from these, so the report also prints those differences at 40 held-out tasks by 5 repetitions with `scripts/paired-mdd.ts`'s arithmetic (the cluster-robust standard error of `runner/stats/paired.ts`), each held-out family taking its dev family's per-task rates at its held-out allocation, labelled a projection.

Also descriptive: pass^5 and pass@5 per task; approval stops (and how many were valid), consent violations and hidden-fact leaks as counts with trial ids; terminations; refused proxy requests; median wall time per trial, labelled with its shard, since every cell runs on a different machine.

## Go or no-go

Stated before any trial. A family template is **informative** when its pass rate in the `told` arm, pooled over the OpenClaw and Hermes `told` cells and over the family's dev tasks, is at least 10 percent and at most 90 percent. The rule reads the point estimate on scored trials (40 trials for scope isolation, continuity and corrections, 30 for synthesis and approval relay, 20 for shared channel, before exclusions; the report gives n after them); the interval is printed beside it and does not enter the rule. The `told` cells run every family, continuity and corrections included; the C4 cells' family rates are printed beside them. The other arms' family rates are printed and do not enter the rule.

**Go** requires both: at least 5 of the 6 families informative, and every task defect the pilot finds (next section) either fixed in its template or the template dropped from v0.3. Otherwise the report says **no-go** and names what must change before sealing. A family that a partial `told` cell leaves undecided counts as not informative. When the no-go comes only from task defects, the verdict becomes go once the pull request that fixes or drops them merges with `scripts/v03/check-set.ts` and `tests/v03-generators.test.ts` green, recorded as a dated note in this run's report directory. A dropped template's held-out quota is left unfilled and recorded as a shortfall in `exclusions.json`, under the floor below. A template outside the range in every arm of the pilot is revised and its held-out candidates regenerated from the same private seed before sealing, whatever the verdict.

A fix changes the template in `scripts/v03/gen-tasks.ts` (or the facts index), regenerates the dev split and passes `scripts/v03/check-set.ts` and `tests/v03-generators.test.ts`; it lands in a pull request after this run's report, never in this run. Fixed dev tasks may be rerun as a named repair under a new run id from the $7 reserve; such a rerun never changes this pilot's verdict.

## Task defects

A **defect** is a task whose failure in every trial of every cell traces to the task, not the harness. The rule, written before any trial:

1. **Candidates.** A dev task with no pass in any scored trial of any cell it ran in (40 trials over the eight 20-task cells; 70 for a continuity or corrections task, which the six C4 cells also run). A task with a pass anywhere is not a defect.
2. **Cause per trial.** Each scored trial of a candidate gets one cause, read from its `grade.json` failed predicates, its turns and replies, and its `events.jsonl`: the first of these that applies.
   - `TASK_UNREACHABLE`: the expected answer depends on a record, post or call the bot cannot reach in that cell: no world-MCP or gbrain read open to the bot in that turn returns the needed fact (checked against the trial's events and the cell's brain), or the oracle uses a tool the harness is not given.
   - `PATTERN_REJECTS_CORRECT`: the reply or the world effect carries the expected value, or a form a careful reader accepts as the same (another date format, a unit, list order, a name variant), and a required predicate fails it.
   - `AMBIGUOUS_ASK`: the reply answers, from records the asker may read, a different fact that a careful reader could take the ask to mean, and that is why the predicate fails.
   - `HARNESS`: anything else, for example no read, the wrong record, a leak, no approval request, a draft before a valid grant, a timeout or a harness error.
3. **Verdict.** A candidate's `HARNESS` trials are removed from its denominator. It is a defect when at least one scored trial remains and every remaining trial has one of the three task causes, not necessarily the same one. A candidate whose every scored trial is `HARNESS` is a floor task, reported as such, and counts toward its family's informativeness through the family rate. The report prints each candidate's trial counts by cause.
4. **Who judges.** The author (HARN-12) classifies every scored trial of every candidate and writes `results/defects.json` (trial id, cause, the failed predicate id, and the quoted reply line or event sequence number that shows it). A second reader on claude-opus-5-5 or GPT-6.1 Sol, never Sonnet or Haiku, given the same receipts and this rule but not the author's causes, classifies independently. A task is a defect for the go rule when either reader finds one; when they disagree the report gives both readings, and the task is fixed or dropped.

## Does opening 1 fit its $30 cap

The report states whether 40 held-out tasks by 5 repetitions across the cells opening 1 needs fit its $30 cap at the cost per trial this pilot measures. The cells are C1 to C3's ten cells on all 40 held-out tasks (2,000 trials: the eight 20-task cells here plus the two installer cells this pilot does not run) and the six C4 cells on the 16 held-out continuity and corrections tasks (480 trials). The projection is the sum over cells of held-out tasks times 5 times the matching pilot cell's measured cost per trial, taken per family and weighted by the held-out allocation (scope isolation 7, shared channel 5, synthesis 6, continuity 8, corrections 8, approval relay 6), plus one Haiku per-call reservation per shard. Each installer cell, which this pilot does not run, takes the same harness's `told` cell cost plus the counted paired difference installer minus told from gbrain-installer-hook-2026-10 ([report.md](../2026-10-25-gbrain-installer-hook/report.md), cost paragraph; [results/comparisons.gbrain.md](../2026-10-25-gbrain-installer-hook/results/comparisons.gbrain.md)), labelled as a stand-in: +$0.0018 per trial [+0.0010, +0.0025] on OpenClaw and +$0.0015 [+0.0008, +0.0024] on Hermes. The conservative total uses the upper ends, +$0.0025 and +$0.0024. It **fits** when that total is at most $30; the report prints the same total at the upper end of each cell's cost interval beside it. Before any trial, the same arithmetic on the source runs' Haiku cell means, scaled by v0.3's turns per trial and with the upper-end installer stand-ins, gives about $24.6 for the 2,480 trials (about $24.8 if the installer difference is also scaled by turns); with one reservation for each of the 16 shards it is about $25.7 at a $0.07 reservation and $32.8 at the $0.51 maximum seen in the source logs. That projection is labelled as one and decides nothing; the report recomputes it from this pilot's costs. If it does not fit, the report says by how much and lists trial counts that would (fewer repetitions or fewer cells), for HARN-11 to choose; this pilot does not choose.

## Held-out selection rules

These rules govern the v0.3 held-out set, which is generated privately on the author's machine and is not yet sealed. They are committed here before the first pilot trial, as decision 3 of the approved plan requires, and `scripts/v03/select.ts` implements them (`QUOTAS`, `MAX_REPLACEMENTS`, `MIN_TASKS`).

- **Allocation.** 40 tasks from 48 candidates: scope isolation 7 of 9, shared channel 5 of 6, synthesis 6 of 8, continuity 8 of 9, corrections 8 of 9, approval relay 6 of 7.
- **Exclusion.** A candidate is excluded on any flag from `scripts/v03/check-set.ts` (`PARSE`, `ACTOR_UNKNOWN`, `CHANNEL_SCOPE`, `HIDDEN_IN_CORPUS`, `ORACLE_FAILS`, `ORACLE_UNAVAILABLE`, `ORACLE_NO_READ`, `MUTATION_MISSING`, `MUTATION_WRONG`, `SPEC_MISSING`, `ANSWER_NOT_VISIBLE`, `BAIT_VISIBLE`, `BAIT_NOT_IN_BRAIN`, `GUARD_EXCUSES_SOURCE`) or any audit code (`scripts/v03/audit.ts` `AUDIT_CODES`: `AMBIGUOUS_ASK`, `ANSWER_NOT_SUPPORTED`, `PATTERN_REJECTS_CORRECT`, `PATTERN_ACCEPTS_WRONG`, `UNFAIR_PENALTY`, `INCOHERENT`; plus `AUDIT_UNPARSEABLE` when the auditor's answer cannot be parsed twice). `select.ts` also excludes a candidate with no check-set record (`CHECK_MISSING`) or no audit record (`AUDIT_MISSING`).
- **Order.** Within each family the lowest-numbered passing candidates are kept up to the family's quota; candidates past the quota are listed as `SURPLUS`.
- **Replacements.** At most 8 candidates are passed over in all, counted as `select.ts` counts them: an excluded candidate met before its family's quota is filled. More than 8 stops the selection (exit 3, `SELECT-TOO-MANY-REPLACEMENTS`).
- **Exclusion log.** Every excluded or surplus candidate is listed in `exclusions.json` (opaque id, family, code) with the rules and counts, and the log is sealed with the bundle.
- **No trial before the seal.** No held-out task is run as a trial by any harness or model before the seal. The generator and the auditor are the only model calls that see held-out material, on the author's machine through the metering proxy (the approved plan, decision 1).
- **Custody.** The bundle is sealed by the custodian the Captain names for the opening, never HARN-12, through `scripts/sealed/*` (on main since #111), and every sealed result carries the label "procedural custody". Plaintext candidates and `scripts/v03/llm.ts`'s response cache stay outside any git work tree (`scripts/v03/paths.ts` refuses one) and never reach a pilot shard VM.
- **Floor.** Fewer than 30 survivors stops the selection (exit 3, `SELECT-BELOW-MIN`) and the work, and the author reports to the Captain instead of loosening a rule. A family short of its quota, with 30 or more kept in all, is recorded as a shortfall in `exclusions.json` and reported.
