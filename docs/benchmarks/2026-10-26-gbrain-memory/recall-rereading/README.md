# Recall re-reading of gbrain-memory-2026-10

A re-reading of the counted run's receipts, written 2026-10-27 by internal-task after the run was reported. It is not a counted result, not part of the preregistered analysis, and changes nothing under `../results/` or in `../report.md`. Every count and rate here comes from `bun scripts/recall-audit.ts`, which reads only the committed receipts (`../results/trials.jsonl`, each live trial's `events.jsonl`, `grade.json` and `turns.jsonl`, and `../results/hot-memory-blocks.json`) and the world seed, and writes the four files beside this note: the tables are in [summary.md](summary.md), and the query examples are rows of [calls.jsonl](calls.jsonl). What the failing replies did (the last paragraph of "Why the failing turns failed") was read from those trials' `events.jsonl` and `trials.jsonl` predicates; summary.md lists every failing turn.

## The question

The report found that with a member token and live ingestion, gbrain held the facts the continuity and corrections tasks need, and the harnesses still answered without them. On `cr-05` both of Sofia's posts were ingested in every live trial, and all 13 Sonnet `search` calls under Kwame's token came back with four pages and no saved fact. This re-reading asks the same question of every gbrain read in the six live cells. When the fact a task's predicate needs was already in the brain and the requester's grant could read it, did the reply carry it, and when a trial failed, was it the retrieval, the harness not asking, or the harness not using what came back?

## How it is counted

- **Consumer turn**: a turn whose required predicates need something said earlier in the scenario. Nine of the 15 tasks have one (`cn-05` has two); `scripts/recall-audit.ts` lists each in `NEEDS` with the scenario turn that carries the fact, a pattern over the message text, the fact type (correction, preference or plain fact) and the predicates that fail without it. Tasks whose answers are in the seeded pages (`cn-07`, `cr-06`, `ms-06`, `ms-07`, `ms-08`, `ar-06`; `ar-06`'s authorizers are also in June's turn-1 DM, which Bettina's grant cannot read) and the mirror turns where the right answer is to withhold have none.
- **Needed fact**: an ingested fact (a `gbrain.ingest` event with outcome ok, earlier in the event log than the start of the consumer turn, so the requester's own message on that turn and anything posted during it never count; a fact ingested twice counts once) whose message text matches the pattern, from that scenario turn's conversation (for `ar-04` and `ar-05`, from any conversation). The bot's own acknowledgement restating the fact counts. **Readable** means the fact's source is in the requesting member's grant, computed with `gbrain/build-brain.ts` (`companyGrants` over the member brain's sources, the grants the image minted tokens for).
- **Returned**: the needed fact's id is among the saved facts the world's tap recorded for that read (`effect.facts`), or, for Hermes only, it was in gbrain's `_meta.brain_hot_memory` block. The tap does not record that block. For Hermes, `hot-memory-blocks.json` gives each block's fact counts without ids, so the fact is counted as returned only when the block is for the requester's private source and its member-written and bot-written counts equal what that source held at that moment (every block in this run was for `private-<requester>`). For OpenClaw and QM the block is not recorded, and their MCP clients do not pass `_meta` to the model (Limits), so their recall is saved facts only: a fact that came back only in a hot-memory block never reached the model.
- **Two oracles**: **original fact returned** counts a read when a member's own message carrying the needed fact came back; **information returned (original or a correct assistant restatement)** also counts the bot's message restating it. Recall hit and miss, the misses and the turn outcomes below use the second; both are reported wherever recall is. The two differ only on `ar-04` and `ar-05`: in 39 calls only the bot's restatement came back (18 of `ar-04`'s 44 calls, 21 of `ar-05`'s 22), and in all 22 `ar-05` calls the original, Kwame's DM, was not readable under June's grant, so the first oracle cannot count them. Each of those restatements was read: each states the draft to Mateo or the $1,480 rate as the original did.
- **Recall** is per call, over search, query and recall calls at a consumer turn while a needed fact was in the brain and readable: returned over calls, by each oracle. The intervals are 95 percent task-clustered bootstrap intervals (tasks resampled with replacement, seed 7, 10,000 draws); with nine tasks they are wide, and a group of fewer than three tasks has none (a single task, the `synthesis` family with one, the `approval_relay` family and the preference fact type with two): a bootstrap over two tasks can only return the two task rates and their pool.

## What it found

The six live cells made 870 gbrain reads (820 `search`, 21 `recall`, 16 `query`, 11 `get_page`, one `list_pages`, one `entity`). 308 of them were a search, query or recall at a consumer turn while the needed fact was in the brain and readable. The original fact came back in 112 of 308 (36 percent, [17, 55] task-clustered). The information came back (the original or a correct assistant restatement) in 151 of 308 (49 percent, [26, 71]), and in 122 (40 percent) through saved facts alone.

Recall depends on the task far more than on the model or the harness ([summary.md](summary.md) has every table):

| fact type | tasks | calls | original fact returned | information returned (original or restatement) | information through saved facts |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| correction | `cr-05`, `cr-07`, `cr-08` | 94 | 32 (34%) [4, 85] | 32 (34%) [4, 85] | 32 |
| preference | `cn-05`, `cn-08` | 72 | 30 (42%), no interval (two tasks) | 30 (42%), no interval (two tasks) | 3 |
| plain fact | `cn-06`, `ms-05`, `ar-04`, `ar-05` | 142 | 50 (35%) [0, 63] | 89 (63%) [19, 91] | 87 |

- Corrections: `cr-08`'s correction came back in 28 of 33 calls, `cr-07`'s in 3 of 33 and `cr-05`'s in 1 of 28 (an OpenClaw Haiku `search` for "Pellow seat count renewal"). The 13 Sonnet `cr-05` searches the report counted are all here, every one with no saved fact.
- Preferences told in a DM came back through saved facts in 3 of 72 calls. The 27 more that count as returned are Hermes's hot-memory blocks, which is how Hermes moved `cn-05` and `cn-08` off the floor. OpenClaw's and QM's MCP clients do not show that block to the model, so for them a DM preference reached the model only as a saved fact.
- A plain fact posted in a channel, `ms-05`'s VT-55120, came back in 0 of 37 calls; every `ms-05` live trial still named it, and all 30 re-read the incident channel that turn, which is where the ticket was.
- By model, information returned: Sonnet 71 of 134 (53 percent, [26, 79]) and Haiku 80 of 174 (46 percent, [25, 66]); original fact returned: Sonnet 51 of 134 (38 percent, [16, 61]) and Haiku 61 of 174 (35 percent, [17, 52]). By harness and model, information returned: 33 to 47 percent for OpenClaw and QM (intervals [13, 53] and [14, 73] for OpenClaw Haiku and Sonnet, [11, 67] and [14, 86] for QM) and 68 to 71 percent for Hermes ([43, 93] Haiku, [33, 92] Sonnet); original fact returned: 24 to 39 percent for OpenClaw and QM ([7, 40] and [4, 46] for OpenClaw Haiku and Sonnet, [0, 62] and [9, 71] for QM) and 52 to 53 percent for Hermes ([22, 79] Haiku, [17, 83] Sonnet). The intervals overlap. Through saved facts alone Hermes returned the information in 40 of 99 calls, OpenClaw in 48 of 129 and QM in 34 of 80, so the gap in the point estimates is the hot-memory block, which only Hermes passes to the model; this is no ranking of the harnesses.

Of the 157 calls that missed, 117 returned saved facts. Of the 117 misses that returned saved facts, 115 included the asker's own current message and in 111 it was the only fact returned. The receipts do not say why a query missed, and no simple rule over the query words fits them. In `cr-08`, "Orlavine seats" returned Gideon's correction in 18 of 18 calls and the five queries naming Ruth Calloway returned it in 0 of 5; in `cr-05`, the one hit was "Pellow seat count renewal", although neither "renews at 52 seats" nor "the signed Pellow form says 46 seats" has the word "renewal", while "Pellow seats renewal" missed every time. The keyless recall rung (`scripts/gbrain-check.ts --rungs recall`, its own pull request) replays these exact queries against a brain rebuilt from the run's ingested posts and records what comes back.

## Why the failing turns failed

299 consumer turns ran in completed live trials; in 160 the needed predicates passed. The 139 that failed:

| outcome | turns | where |
|---|---:|---|
| the harness made no gbrain read that turn | 28 | `cn-05` 19 (all turn 4, Kwame's question right after Bettina's), `cn-08` 6, `cr-05` 3 |
| the needed fact was not readable under the requester's grant | 8 | `ar-05`: June approves, and the rate was only in Kwame's DM, which June's grant does not read, by design |
| retrieval miss, certain | 25 | `cr-05`: 10 Hermes, 15 OpenClaw and QM; a channel correction, which a hot-memory block (private source only) never carries |
| retrieval miss in saved facts, hot memory not shown to the model | 37 | `cn-05` 23, `cn-08` 13, `cn-06` 1, all OpenClaw and QM, all DM facts |
| returned, and the predicate still failed | 41 | `cn-06` 19, `ar-04` and `ar-05` 14, `cn-05` 5, `cn-08` 3 |

So in 62 of the 139 the needed fact did not reach the model through a gbrain reply. In 25 no reply could have carried it other than as a saved fact, and in 37 saved facts missed it and OpenClaw's or QM's MCP client dropped any hot-memory block gbrain sent with the reply (Limits); the receipts do not say whether gbrain sent one in those sessions, and where it did, the fact was retrieved and then lost on the harness side. The other harness-side losses are counted outright: 41 consumer turns got the fact back and still failed the predicate, and 28 made no gbrain read. The 41 are not all "ignored": in `cn-06` all 19 created the meeting without Kwame's contact in the notes (16 with the right attendees, 3 also leaving Kwame off) (the contact came from his DM, and Bettina is an attendee; rule 2 of the preamble can be read to forbid it, the task scores it as a failure); in `ar-04` and `ar-05`, 11 made no draft after the approval and 3 drafted the mail to Mateo with Gideon copied, which the task's only-these-recipients predicate fails; in `cn-05` 5 Haiku replies wrote the ISO date; in `cn-08` 3 Hermes Haiku bookings added June and dropped Ruth.

`cr-05` is the clean case. In 25 of its 30 live trials the correction was in the brain, readable by Kwame, and not in the reply; in 3 more the harness did not search; 2 passed.

## Limits

- Hot memory in OpenClaw and QM, read from the code and not from a receipt. At the #87 pin gbrain puts the hot-memory block only in the tool result's `_meta` and leaves `content` as it was (`src/mcp/dispatch.ts` lines 148-164, the `ToolResult` type, whose comment says MCP hosts generally do not show `_meta` to the model, and lines 1167-1174, the injection; read in the `harness-eval/gbrain` image built at `b06ac32b`). The world's tap hands gbrain's reply body to the harness unchanged (`world/services/gbrain-tap.ts` lines 321 and 345), and this repository registers gbrain with each harness as a plain MCP server and patches neither client (`harnesses/openclaw/adapter.sh` lines 259-261, `harnesses/qm/adapter.sh` lines 106-108; the memory cells set no `gbrain.install`, so no gbrain plugin or hook ran). OpenClaw 2026.10.6 builds the model's tool result in `projectMcpCallToolResult` (`src/agents/mcp-content.ts`, lines 107-149 of `dist/mcp-content-D63Cg6c1.mjs` in the npm package) from `content` and `structuredContent` only, and passes every MCP tool result through it (`dist/agent-bundle-mcp-materialize-Bm-FVXhI.mjs` line 376, on the raw result that `dist/agents/agent-bundle-mcp-runtime.js` lines 709-726 return); its code-mode path deletes `_meta` outright (line 51 of the same `mcp-content` file). QM v0.1.15 (commit `7417aa2a`, the Pi inner harness the memory cells ran) turns an MCP result into the tool's text with `mcpResultText`, which joins the text items of `content` (`src/mcp/mcp-client.ts` lines 91-98), falling back to `structuredContent` (`src/mcp/mcp-tool-service.ts` line 209), and the Pi tool returns that text (`src/harness/agent-tools.ts` lines 4176-4187). Neither reads `_meta`, so a hot-memory block gbrain sent to OpenClaw or QM was not shown to the model. This settles what the clients would forward, not whether gbrain sent a block on a given read: the tap records no block for these two harnesses, so its presence on any OpenClaw or QM read is unknown, and either way it did not reach the model. The 37 turns labelled "hot memory not shown to the model" are retrieval misses as the model saw them; the share of them where gbrain did send the fact in a block, a loss on the harness side, cannot be counted from the receipts. Hermes is the contrast: its own session store held the block in 158 of its 278 gbrain tool results (the report's "What the brain returned, and what the tap saw").
- `NEEDS` is a reading of the sealed task files, and its patterns decide what counts as the needed fact; a bot acknowledgement that restates the fact counts, one that garbles it does not. The per-call table (`calls.jsonl`) lists the needed fact ids, the returned ids and the pages, so any row can be checked against `events.jsonl`.
- Recall is over calls, and calls cluster by trial and task, so a harness that searches three times on one turn contributes three calls. `turns.jsonl` is the per-turn view.
- This is the run at gbrain `b06ac32b` (the #87 pin). It says nothing about a later gbrain commit.

## Files

- [calls.jsonl](calls.jsonl): one row per gbrain read in a live cell (870): task, harness, model, repetition, trial pass, seq, turn, grant, tool, query and arguments, the needed fact (in brain, readable), saved facts and pages returned, which saved facts were the requester's own message on that turn, the hot-memory block (Hermes; "not shown to the model" for OpenClaw and QM), and recall hit, miss or n/a by each oracle (`recall_original` for the original fact, `recall` for the information).
- [turns.jsonl](turns.jsonl): one row per live trial and consumer turn (300; the one `harness_error` trial's turn is marked excluded): requester, needed fact, gbrain reads and queries, whether the information (`returned_needed`) and the original (`returned_original`) came back, whether the harness re-read the source conversation (`conversations.history` or `replies` on that channel, by id or name), and the outcome above.
- [summary.json](summary.json) and [summary.md](summary.md): the tables, with both oracles named in `oracles`.

`bun scripts/recall-audit.ts --cases <file>` also writes the case list the keyless recall rung replays (its own pull request): one case per scored search, query or recall call, with `kind` (miss or control, by the information oracle), `original_returned`, `question` (the requester's message at the consumer turn), `needed_ids` (the readable originals) and `restatement_ids` (the readable assistant restatements the second oracle accepts), both as fact ids of the counted run's sidecar, and `needed` (each of those facts' channel, ts, author and source).

Regenerate with `bun scripts/recall-audit.ts` from the repository root; `tests/recall-audit.test.ts` checks all four committed files match a fresh run and that the headline counts, the misses and the outcome table above are the ones it computes.
